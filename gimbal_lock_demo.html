Rotations have 3 degrees of freedom, but Euler angle parametrizations can lose one when two rotation axes align. This is gimbal lock.

What's Happening

Move the sliders to see how yaw (Y), pitch (X), and roll (Z) compose. The innermost gimbal carries your camera. When pitch ≈ ±90°, the outer yaw ring and inner roll ring align — you lose a degree of freedom.

The Math

Euler rotations compose as matrix multiplication. For YXZ order:

R = RY(ψ) · RX(θ) · RZ(φ)

When θ → ±π/2, the first column of RX becomes (0, ±1, 0)ᵀ, causing the Y and Z rotation axes to align.

RY · RX(±π/2) = RY · [axis alignment]
→ ψ and φ affect the same direction

The Fix

Quaternions represent rotations without intermediate axes. They maintain 3 DOF everywhere and interpolate smoothly.

q = qyaw ⊗ qpitch ⊗ qroll
